While writing this, I wasn't trying to answer whether AI can do engineering. It clearly can do significant portions of it, and pretending otherwise is cope.

The real question is how a trained engineer maintains an absolute premium in a world where immediate analysis, literature reviews, parameter sweeps, and report drafts can all be generated in seconds.

The answer I try to derive is specific and defensible. It comes directly from the dirt-under-the-fingernails experience of doing the other projects in this portfolio, alongside the engineering and management leadership roles I've taken so far. 

True leverage belongs to the physical intuition that tells you which calculation actually matters, or the gut check that tells you a simulation result seems wrong before you even run the verification, which only happens when you have actually built things in the physical world instead of just prompting your way through them.
